# GikaiLens: Exploring Historical Japanese Parliamentary Debates with Generative AI

My idea for the capstone project is to develop a research assistant for exploring the proceedings of the Imperial Japanese Diet (帝国議会, 1890–1947). I have previously worked with this corpus for my bachelor's thesis, in which I examined the historical use of the term 社会 (*shakai*, "society") in parliamentary speeches. When working with such a large collection of historical texts, it is particularly difficult to find relevant passages and compare how different speakers discussed similar topics. I would therefore like to explore how generative AI could support this process.

I want to build a tool that allows users to search for parliamentary speeches on topics they are interested in and then analyse or compare the results. For example, a user could ask how different parliamentarians discussed political participation during the 1920s. The system would retrieve relevant speeches, provide links to the original sources and use an LLM to generate summaries or comparisons. I would also like to include the option of translating passages from Japanese into English or German. If time permits, another feature could allow users to upload academic literature and incorporate it into the analysis, while distinguishing between primary and secondary sources.

The main source will be the *Database System for the Minutes of the Imperial Diet*, provided by the National Diet Library of Japan. Since the database offers an API, I hope to retrieve the speeches and their metadata directly. For the initial prototype, I plan to work with a smaller selection of speeches, potentially focusing on debates surrounding the extension of voting rights in 1925. When working with the proceedings of the Imperial Japanese Diet, however, it is important to keep in mind that they mainly represent those who were able to participate in parliamentary debates, rather than Japanese society as a whole. Depending on the question, the research assistant should therefore also indicate when the available sources are insufficient to provide a reliable answer.

Technically, I plan to use retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) to ground the LLM's responses in the historical sources. Since this is my first project involving RAG, I would prefer to focus on a relatively simple and transparent system rather than implementing a more complex agentic workflow. I also want to compare how a regular LLM, an improved prompt and a RAG-based system perform on the same historical questions.

The tool would primarily be intended for students and researchers interested in modern Japanese history. One concern is the reliability of its results, particularly given the OCR errors in the digitised sources and the difficulties of interpreting historical Japanese. I therefore want users to be able to check the original passages rather than having to rely entirely on AI-generated interpretations.

I plan to work on this project individually.
